# Foreign intervention and influence: mechanism matrix

This is an evidence map, not a political verdict. It distinguishes the state/institution, date, channel, documented action, evidence status, and missing links. Foreign involvement in Indonesia changed by period and could be contradictory within one government. An earlier alliance or assistance program is not proof of participation in the September 1965 events.

## Cross-cutting tests

1. **Provenance:** Who authored the document, when, for which audience and with what access?
2. **Action stage:** Is the evidence for a proposal, authorization, payment, transfer, deployment, or effect?
3. **Recipient evidence:** Is there Indonesian confirmation or a record by the local user/target?
4. **Attribution:** Are agency names explicit or redacted? Is a source repeating another source?
5. **Causality:** Does evidence show that foreign action changed a decision or outcome, or only that it co-occurred?
6. **Negative evidence:** A statement such as “no role” is bounded by its author's mandate and scope. It cannot automatically negate activity recorded in other files.
7. **Political judgment:** This matrix supplies verifiable facts and uncertainty only. The author decides what causal and political meaning follows.
## Research update — Japanese occupation and colonial economic foundations (2026-11-08)

- Recent sources strengthen the Japan row: occupation administration used Indonesian nationalist leaders and local institutions for political mobilization while imposing rice quotas, food-movement restrictions, forced labor and military recruitment. Evidence must distinguish policy design, local implementation, Sukarno's participation, and the much wider occupation apparatus. [S001, S016, S062]
- The famine evidence is Java-specific and method-sensitive. The 2024 published demographic study estimates 3.3 million net population loss, not 3.3 million deaths; it apportions roughly 1.8 million excess deaths and 1.4 million missing births. The prior working-paper abstract has different rounded values. [S062, S074]
- Colonial land and labor regimes before 1942 shaped the economy and political field through compulsory crop delivery, land concessions, slavery, indenture and migration. These operated differently across Java, Sumatra and other islands; add region-level sources before drawing national conclusions. [S063–S066]
- ANRI's current record gateways now include Inggit family materials, marriage/divorce files and a 2024 oral-history interview. These are primary/archival leads, not yet reviewed records. [S059, S060, S073]

## 2026-11-10 clarification: Kudus local list versus U.S. list transfer

The original 10 December 1965 U.S. Embassy A-386 reports that Catholic Party coordinator Suhandjo said he had furnished the Indonesian Army a list of over 200 local Baperki members in the Kudus business community; the cable says a separate list of 20 Baperki leaders was slated for intensive questioning and that many had disappeared. This was a local list reported by its alleged compiler. It is **not** the same as the separate PKI and affiliated-organization list compiled by U.S. Embassy staff and acknowledged in FRUS d185 as having been shared with Indonesian officials in December. The two chains have different compilers, recipients and proof: A-386 provides no copy or Army receipt for Suhandjo's list; d185 documents U.S. transfer but does not trace each named person to a specific arrest or killing. Do not merge the lists or infer individual outcomes from either. [S151; S032; S189; C143; C648; C649]
