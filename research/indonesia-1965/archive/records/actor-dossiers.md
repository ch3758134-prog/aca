# Initial actor and relationship dossiers

**Status:** an index for further research, not complete biographies. The aim is to capture relevant public biographical facts and personal/political ties without treating intimacy, kinship, patronage or rumor as proof of political coordination. Record exact dates and source-level disagreements. No private or inaccessible material has been obtained.

## Sukarno / Soekarno (1901–1970)

- **Current sourced facts:** Born in Surabaya on 6 June 1901; Cambridge describes his father as a Javanese teacher from the lower aristocracy and his mother as Balinese; ANRI names them as Raden Soekemi Sosrodihardjo and Ida Ayu Nyoman Rai Srimben. Studied in Surabaya and Bandung; founded PNI in 1927; imprisoned and exiled by Dutch authorities; returned to public political life under Japanese occupation; proclaimed independence with Hatta in 1945; served as president through the revolutionary and post-revolutionary state; imposed Guided Democracy after the 1957 crisis; came under military pressure in 1965–66. [S001, S002]
- **Personal/kinship leads:** Tjokroaminoto's household and his daughter Siti Oetari connect political mentorship, residence, alliance and marriage. Marriage dates with Oetari and Inggit conflict across ANRI/Cambridge summaries. Cambridge lists later marriages to Fatmawati, Hartini, Kartini Manoppo, Ratna Sari Dewi/Naoko Nemoto, Haryati, Yurike Sanger and Heldy Jafar. Treat this as a starting index; verify each relation and date against marriage records, family papers and oral testimony. [S001, S002]
- **Research questions:** parents' origins and social position; education and religious/intellectual formation; household and financial support; Inggit's contribution during exile; relationship with Hatta, Sjahrir, Army leaders, Aidit, Subandrio and foreign envoys; private correspondence; political use and limits of presidential charisma; decision-making during occupation, 1948, Guided Democracy, September–October 1965 and 1966.
- **Caution:** personal ties do not prove political control, complicity or conspiracy. A later memoir is testimony with its own temporal and political position.

## H.O.S. Tjokroaminoto

- **Current sourced relation:** ANRI identifies him as a Sarekat Islam leader and says Sukarno lived in his household while studying; his daughter Siti Oetari married Sukarno. [S001, S002]
- **Unresolved:** exact mentorship and financial relationship, household composition, exchange of ideas, impact of the marriage/divorce, contemporaneous letters and later recollections.
- **Next sources:** Sarekat Islam records, Dutch surveillance files, family papers, Surabaya school/address records, oral histories.

## Siti Oetari

- **Current sourced relation:** Cambridge and ANRI identify her as Tjokroaminoto's daughter and Sukarno's first wife. The marriage year is reported as 1920 or 1921. [S001, S002]
- **Unresolved:** her own biography and agency, divorce date, legal/cultural form of the marriage, contemporaneous family accounts.
- **Next sources:** marriage/divorce registers; Tjokroaminoto family archive; interviews with descendants; local press.

## Inggit Garnasih

- **Current sourced relation:** Cambridge places her marriage to Sukarno in 1922; ANRI timeline gives 24 April 1923 and cites a marriage certificate number. Cambridge notes Sukarno's later exile, but the role of Inggit during exile needs fuller source review. [S001, S002]
- **Unresolved:** prior marriage/divorce, economic support, correspondence, relationship with Sukarno's political work, reasons and timing of separation.
- **Next sources:** certificate referenced by ANRI; Inggit's own memoirs and family testimony; prison/exile correspondence; local records.

## Fatmawati and other spouses

- Cambridge lists Fatmawati (m. 1943), Hartini (1953), Kartini Manoppo (1959), Ratna Sari Dewi/Naoko Nemoto (1962), Haryati (1963), Yurike Sanger (1964) and Heldy Jafar (1967). These dates and biographical labels are a publisher summary, not independently verified in this project. [S001]
- **Research questions:** legal marriage vs customary/political recognition; chronology and overlap; family and palace networks; public/private roles; how reliable later accounts are. Avoid reducing these people to instruments in Sukarno's political story.

## Mohammad Hatta

- Cambridge's biographical summary places Hatta in the Netherlands-based Indonesian student movement, notes his 1927 arrest and acquittal, later collaboration with Sukarno's PNI, and exile with Sjahrir. The source also identifies Sukarno and Hatta as co-proclaimers of independence. [S001]
- **Relationship questions:** cooperation and disagreement in the revolution; Hatta's dealings with army and party; constitutional differences; resignation and later correspondence. Need Hatta papers, Cabinet/KNIP records, interviews and contemporary press before making a full political assessment.

## Sutan Sjahrir and Tan Malaka

- Cambridge identifies Sjahrir as connected to the New PNI with Hatta; modern revolutionary scholarship describes Tan Malaka as a leading critic of negotiated strategy and a participant in competing revolutionary political networks. [S001, S058]
- **Current coverage:** leads only. Reconstruct personal ties, strategic disagreements and relationships to Sukarno and Army formations through their own papers and contemporaneous sources.

## Amir Sjarifuddin and Musso

- Recent scholarship on Madiun and Amir's political life offers competing explanations of how Amir moved into Musso's orbit and the 1948 crisis. A U.S. telegram reproduces Republican allegations; another Cambridge article challenges simple international-command interpretations. [S007–S015]
- **Caution:** record offices and dates separately; distinguish FDR, PKI, pro-left military units and local allies. Avoid assigning one unified intention to every participant.

## Suharto

- A 6 October 1965 U.S. intelligence memorandum records Suharto as Kostrad commander and says he assumed Army command on the evening of 1 October. This is a dated U.S. intelligence account; confirm with Indonesian Army orders, biographies and other foreign cables. [S026]
- **Research questions:** military career before 1965; command relations with Yani, Nasution and Kostrad; territorial forces; personal networks and economic interests; communications and authority after 1 October; later testimony and archive control.

## D.N. Aidit, Sjam Kamaruzaman and Untung

- Current archive coverage has scholarly leads for PKI growth and ANRI's 2025 announcement of PKI archival materials, but no name-level Indonesian dossier yet. [S040, S053]
- The early U.S. intelligence report names Untung as a leader in its initial account; this does not establish the full chain of command. [S026]
- **Unresolved:** Aidit's exact movement and communications; PKI Politburo decisions; Sjam's identity, role and relationship to PKI/Army channels; Untung's unit and orders; source reliability of later trials/confessions and official biographies.
- **Next sources:** ANRI/NARA source publications, Indonesian military and court records, contemporary party papers, diplomatic cables, Chinese/Soviet archives, oral histories, and source criticism of confessions.

## Nasution, Yani, Sukendro and other military/presidential officials

- The actor-level Army chronology is incomplete. FRUS communications documents identify some named intermediaries in covert supply requests, including Sukendro and Colonel Achmad Soemantri; the 1965 intelligence and aid documents should be indexed person-by-person. [S026, S030–S033]
- **Next step:** create dated roles from official appointment records, orders, unit charts, correspondence and independent memoirs; avoid treating a rank or contact as evidence of a clandestine role.

## Foreign intermediaries and private networks

- General diplomatic liaison, private access, payments, procurement cutouts, commercial cover, military training, military attachés, radio/propaganda and intelligence liaison are separate mechanisms. U.S. records mention secure funding to unnamed nationalist personalities in 1965 and covert commercial cover in November; recipients, intermediaries and effects need stronger identification. [S024, S030]
- **Next step:** create separate actor files for Allan Pope, Philip Zorthian, Sukendro, Achmad Soemantri, British IRD officials, Australian diplomats/intelligence officers, Chinese and Soviet diplomats, Japanese business/reparations officials—only where a source documents the connection.

## Relationship confidence

- **High:** a primary record or multiple independent credible sources establish the relationship and approximate date.
- **Moderate:** one credible biography/archive narrative establishes it, but no original record or independent account is checked.
- **Low/open:** hearsay, memoir-only, intelligence inference, prosecutorial confession or a single unverified secondary summary.
- A relationship must be described precisely: family, marriage, mentorship, formal office, command chain, liaison, funding, ideological affiliation or alleged conspiracy.
## Research update — personal records and early networks (2026-11-08)

### Inggit Garnasih and Sukarno

- ANRI reports that a 2024 Bandung archival survey located Inggit's family materials, Sukarno's ITB enrollment register and grades, and interviewed Inggit's grandson. An ANRI archival-management article identifies marriage and divorce documents as surviving personal records. [S059, S060]
- The marriage year is currently best recorded as 1923, with exact day/month unresolved: ANRI's timeline says 24 April and cites certificate No. 1138; a 2010 report attributed to Inggit's heir gives 24 March; Cambridge's short biography says 1922. Do not treat an article paraphrase as equivalent to the original certificate. [S001, S002, S059, S061]
- The archive lead is unusually concrete: request the certificate's item-level description and image, compare the archival original with family-held copies, review the divorce record, and trace Inggit/Garnasih family correspondence and oral-history metadata. [S059, S060]
- The ANRI timeline also provides a lead that Inggit married Sukarno after previously being married; exact prior relationship, legal status and her own perspective need independent records rather than biographical shorthand.

### Hatta and Sukarno

- ANRI's timeline records Hatta's February 1929 letter urging Sukarno to consider restricting activity or leaving temporarily and offering to prepare travel costs and lodging in the Netherlands. This is an unusually specific lead for a private political relationship. The letter's original wording, survival, archival reference, Sukarno's response and later relationship still need verification. [S002]

### Early Sukarno biography and schools

- ANRI's chronology places Sukarno with his grandfather in Tulungagung around 1907; schooling in Mojokerto from 1908; ELS from 1911; and HBS/household at Tjokroaminoto's home in 1915. Cambridge supports the broad Surabaya schooling and nationalist-network context. [S001, S002]
- The Pusdipres 2024 survey reports that the ITB student register and grade records are located; these are potential primary checks for education and class trajectory, not yet inspected documents. [S060]
- Sukarno's autobiography, as told to Cindy Adams, is a potentially valuable first-person source for family, occupation and political relationships, but must be treated as a crafted and mediated political self-narrative. The copy has not yet been read. [S069]

### Relationships remain differentiated

The early left had connections among ISDV/PKI, Sarekat Islam factions, youth organizations, labor and nationalist study groups. ANRI's timeline records Sukarno defending Tjokroaminoto in a 1923 meeting involving PKI and SI Merah, and earlier links through Jong Java and Sarekat Islam. These are meeting/organizational facts; they do not establish ideological identity or durable alliance. [S002, S040, S070]

## Nasution, Sukarno and the Army in Guided Democracy (1957–1960)

- U.S. papers show the Army Chief of Staff Nasution as a central interlocutor in the U.S. military-aid program. One January 1959 recommendation explicitly proposed strengthening Nasution and officers it characterized as pro-West; later program documents state that most U.S. equipment went to the Army for internal security and anti-Communist purposes. This is evidence of U.S. priorities, not proof that Nasution or the Army simply followed U.S. direction. [S075, S088–S090]
- U.S. assessments describe the Army as expanding its government role after the July 1959 Cabinet formation and Sukarno as using it as a counterweight to the PKI. The latter is an embassy interpretation; compare Indonesian appointment decrees, military councils, Army doctrine and Sukarno's own speeches. [S078]
- The December 1957–1958 Dutch-company takeover tied military authority to economic management: union occupation, military supervision, government boards, Indonesian managers and later legal nationalization are separate links. Company-level control and Army business networks remain untraced. [S079–S082; S051]

## Peasant, plantation-labor and women's organizations

- The BTI, SARBUPRI and Gerwani should be analyzed as related but distinct organizations. The current scholarly synthesis reports BTI's mostly Java-based rural following, SARBUPRI's plantation-worker base in and beyond Java, and Gerwani's combination of agrarian, wage, debt and women's political demands. Membership numbers are organization claims reported by scholarship, not independently verified counts. [S041]
- PKI/BTI land-reform strategy in the 1950s and early 1960s was shaped by an effort to mobilize a heterogeneous peasantry around tenancy reform and redistribution, rather than a single undifferentiated collectivization program. The 1961 PKI peasant conference transcript is evidence of public party demands; its original printed source remains to be located. [S041, S092]
- By 1963–64, dispute over unilateral action and law implementation also exposed limits and conflicts within Sukarno's balancing politics: this is a scholarly account, and the June 1964 directive, September speech and local episodes still need primary records. [S041]

## Masyumi and PSI

- U.S. officials reported the two parties excluded from the Guided Democracy executive in 1959; decrees dated 17 August 1960 ordered their dissolution. The decrees establish formal action but do not establish the full range of prior opposition, alleged involvement in regional rebellion, legality, or local enforcement. [S078, S086–S087]


## Sukarno: personal and political network leads

- ANRI's curated Presidentpedia names Raden Soekemi Sosrodihardjo and Ida Ayu Nyoman Rai as Sukarno's parents and reports his Surabaya schooling and residence in H.O.S. Tjokroaminoto's household before HBS and THS. These details should be checked against school registers, family letters and household records; they do not by themselves establish the precise nature or direction of political influence. [S093; S001–S002]
- ANRI's September 2024 report says its team located Inggit Garnasih family-archive materials, Sukarno's ITB student register and grade records and interviewed Inggit's grandson. The archival objects and oral-history transcript still require item-level review. [S060; S073]
- The U.S. NSC's 1953 policy directive explicitly targeted personal influence on Indonesian leaders, especially Sukarno, while its 1961 staff retrospective framed U.S. policy as an attempt to influence Sukarno's political balancing. These are statements of U.S. intention/interpretation, not independent proof that Sukarno was controlled or that a policy succeeded. [S095; S097]


## Research update — personal and civil-military networks before 1965

### Sukarno, Nasution and Sultan Hamengkubuwono IX

- In a 22 October 1952 meeting memorandum, Ambassador Cochran says Sukarno characterized the Palace crowd as a mixture of young Army officers/civilians associated with or influenced by PSI, Murba members and people favoring dictatorship under Sukarno. Sukarno reportedly said the Irian issue helped disperse the crowd and expressed respect and affection for Sultan Hamengkubuwono IX, while disputing press stories of a personal quarrel. Preserve this as Sukarno's reported account and political self-presentation; compare the Sultan's own files and Indonesian reporting. [S105]
- An October 20 cable describes the chain through which President's Secretary Pringgo Digdo supplied the U.S. ambassador an inside account of the October 17 events, including the Army petition and the Sultan's reported reaction. The source explicitly asked for limited distribution to protect Pringgo; the record is both useful and interested/secondhand. [S104]

### Nasution and Zulkifli Lubis; Army-U.S. contacts

- A 1958 secret U.S. profile records Nasution's birth and training history, the October 17 affair, his 1953–55 inactive period, his role in IPKI and his return to the Army chief-of-staff position. It also says U.S. military officers had been on close friendly terms with him during his earlier tenure, and that Army training in U.S. schools began under him. These details identify personal and institutional channels to verify against Indonesian service records, correspondence and memoirs; the U.S. profile's political and psychological labels are not adopted as facts about motive. [S114; S096]
- The same profile identifies Zulkifli Lubis as Nasution's first cousin and attributes part of their antagonism and subsequent decisions to a personal rivalry dating at least to October 17. The kinship can be checked; the motive is an uncorroborated U.S. analytic assertion. Seek family records, Army appointment/removal records, correspondence and both men's writings before drawing any causal inference. [S114]

### Hatta and Sukarno

- Later publications reproduce Hatta's July 1956 letters as arguing that the elected DPR and Constituent Assembly had taken office, and that the vice-presidency was unnecessary within a parliamentary cabinet system. The Constitutional Court's historical volume separately cites Mavis Rose for political differences with Sukarno; the original letters and Cabinet/DPR record should be inspected before assigning relative weight to the constitutional and political reasons. [S107; S113]
- The 26 December 1956 U.S. embassy telegram portrays Hatta as resting above Bogor and worried about his wife's illness, yet still consulting Simatupang and leaders from Masjumi, PNI, NU and several smaller parties during the Sumatra crisis. It records Hatta's preference for Sukarno to form a presidential cabinet. The cable is a paraphrased foreign diplomatic report; it offers a relationship map, not a verbatim or complete explanation of Hatta's actions. [S106]
