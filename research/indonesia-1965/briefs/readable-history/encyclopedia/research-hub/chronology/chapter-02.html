Research update — second pass (2026-11-08)

该段11项可定位日期记录

Colonial land and labor backstory added

1830 1830 onward: Scholarship locates the Dutch Cultivation System primarily in Java, where colonial authorities extracted peasant land and labor for cash-crop delivery. It did not produce one uniform class structure across the archipelago; plantation labor, slavery, indenture, village land, tenancy and migration differed by region and period. [S063–S066]

1870 1870: A recent agrarian synthesis describes the Agrarian Law's designation of uncultivated or shifting-cultivation lands without recognized private rights as colonial state land and its role in enabling long leases and plantations. This is a scholarly summary; statutory text and local concession records remain to be read. [S063]

时期未定 19th-century mortality: A peer-reviewed study estimates adverse mortality effects of forced labor under the Java Cultivation System for 1834–1879. Its Java-specific model cannot be generalized to all Indonesia or directly mapped onto the 1940s. [S066]

1914 1914–1920: Scholarly references trace the ISDV to 1914 and the PKI name to 1920; Indonesian left organizing also involved Sarekat Islam, labor and intellectual networks. A party-centered account is incomplete, and party constitutions/newspapers have not yet been examined. [S040, S070]

1920 1920–1921: ANRI says Sukarno married Siti Oetari in April 1920 and notes another source gives 1921. Cambridge gives 1921. [S001, S002]

1922 1922–1923: The Inggit marriage date has a source conflict. Cambridge gives 1922; ANRI's archival timeline gives 24 April 1923 and cites marriage certificate No. 1138; a report based on the family-held certificate says 24 March 1923. Best present assessment: 1923 is better supported; exact date unresolved pending inspection of the document. [S001, S002, S059–S061]

1929 1929: ANRI's timeline records a letter from Hatta advising Sukarno to restrain political activity or leave temporarily, and offering to arrange travel costs and lodging in the Netherlands. This provides a specific personal/political network lead; the letter itself remains to be read. [S002]

1944 1944–1945: A new peer-reviewed demographic article estimates a Java-only net population loss of 3.3 million during 1942–45, separated into 1.8 million excess deaths and about 1.4 million missing births. Its earlier working-paper abstract gives rounded 3.4 million and 1.9 million excess deaths. An older 4-million figure includes speculative indirect losses in 1946. These are method-dependent demographic estimates, not a precise victim count. [S016, S062, S074]

2024 Food policy and famine: The 2024 study links delayed harvest after drought to Japanese rice quotas, below-market compensation eroded by inflation, and restrictions on inter-district food shipment. It relies on incomplete birth and mortality records and notes under-registration of infant mortality. [S062]

时期未定 Romusha: Estimates have different denominators. One study cites 2.6 million Java laborers and at least 200,000 deaths; another commonly quoted figure concerns about 300,000 transported overseas and about 77,000 survivors. These cannot be combined until the underlying source populations and transport routes are verified. [S062, S068]

1965 Sukarno's own retrospective: A recent historiographical article reports a 1965 statement in which he took responsibility for romusha suffering while defending wartime cooperation as part of the path to independence. Exact speech, original wording and context remain unverified. [S016]

Relationship and archive leads

ANRI's 2024 Bandung search report says it located Inggit Garnasih family-archive material, Sukarno's ITB student register and grade records, and interviewed Inggit's grandson. ANRI's archive-journal article separately describes the marriage/divorce documents as personal archival records. These create a concrete route for expanding Sukarno's education, household, marriage and exile biographies; no underlying family file or interview transcript is yet in the claim evidence. [S059, S060, S073]

Source-revision note

This pass changed the working confidence for the Inggit marriage year from conflicting 1922/1923 summaries to “1923 better supported, exact date unresolved.” It also separated Java net population loss from excess deaths and missing births, and separated working-paper estimates from the published article. The prior commit retains the first-pass record for audit.

Research update — third pass (2026-11-08 UTC)

该段11项可定位日期记录

1957 5–6 Dec 1957: Workers had taken over KPM and other Dutch businesses. The Cabinet decision reported to the U.S. Embassy placed them under government control and management boards; Foreign Ministry Secretary General Suwito described this as temporary protective custody, not yet confiscation/nationalization. This is an attributed diplomatic report of his explanation, not a Cabinet transcript. [S079]

1957 1957–1958: The documentary sequence includes worker action, military supervision, central government management bodies and later formal nationalization. PP 10/1958 created a central body for Dutch industrial/mining enterprises and Indonesian managers; PP 23/24 placed Dutch firms under government control in April 1958; Law 86/1958 provided for nationalization of designated companies, compensation and Supreme Court appeals, while its official record gives an effective date of 3 Dec 1957. These are legally distinct states. [S051, S080–S082]

1958 1958–1961 U.S. military assistance: FRUS papers record an initial $7m increment essentially shipped by December 1958, a later $15m Army shipment program, FY1960 authorization up to $20m with $15m programmed, and later program ceilings of $22m/$20m/$21m for FY1959/60/61. U.S. policy papers explicitly connect aid priorities to internal security, countering Communist activity and supporting Nasution/pro-West officers. Program ceiling, shipments, Indonesian receipt, use and effect remain separate questions. [S075, S088–S090]

1959 20 Feb–5 Jul 1959: A U.S. memo reported Sukarno's announcement of a Cabinet plan to return to the 1945 Constitution and include functional groups; the July 5 decree later dissolved the Constituent Assembly and restored that Constitution. On 10 July the first Guided Democracy Cabinet took office. U.S. officials described the Army's role as expanding and the PKI's executive role as limited; their analysis of Sukarno's balancing strategy remains a contemporary U.S. interpretation. [S036, S077–S078]

1960 7 Jan 1960: Law 2/1960 regulated food-crop sharecropping through written agreements before village authorities, district approval, minimum terms and district-set harvest shares. [S038, S084]

1960 17 Aug 1960: Presidential Decrees 200 and 201 formally dissolved Masyumi and PSI. The official BPK record and ANRI-attributed image establish the decree records; exact grounds, full clauses and branch-level enforcement remain to be transcribed. [S086–S087]

1960 24 Sep 1960: The Basic Agrarian Law replaced named colonial provisions and set principles of social function, limits on holdings, active cultivation and compensated redistribution of surplus land. [S083]

1960 29 Dec 1960 / 1 Jan 1961: Perpu 56/1960 set household landholding limits and a policy goal of a minimum two hectares per farm family, with excess land to be acquired and redistributed under further rules. The same law's explanatory memorandum says about 60% of farmers were landless; this is the government's policy estimate, not an independent census result. [S085]

1961 1961: A scholarly reconstruction describes a committee hierarchy from central to village level, with local administrators and security officials shaping implementation and peasant organizations holding limited seats; verify each district against original rosters and minutes. [S091]

1961 20–24 Apr 1961: A White House briefing identified influence over Sukarno, West New Guinea options and U.S. aid as issues for Kennedy's visit; it described prior U.S. support to the 1957–58 rebellion and the subsequent Army policy in strongly strategic terms. In the recorded meeting, Sukarno directly pressed the United States over West Irian and linked its position to Indonesian national aspirations. Compare the U.S. record with Indonesian notes and later actions. [S097–S098]

1953 1953–1964 agrarian mobilization: Scholarship traces PKI/BTI shift toward private land redistribution, campaigning for the 1960 laws, and distinct Gerwani demands on women's land ownership, village participation, wages and debt. It reports sporadic unilateral land occupations after implementation stalled in 1963, Sukarno's June 1964 prohibition and his September 1964 public welcome to BTI delegates. These are scholarly reconstructions pending original party, presidential and local records. [S041, S092]

Evidence and interpretation note

This pass separates legal authority from company control and land-law passage from implementation. It also distinguishes U.S. policy intentions and authorization ceilings from delivery, Indonesian receipt and downstream effect. Organization membership figures, local conflict and causes of later violence remain open to independent, region-level confirmation.

Research update — fifth pass (2026-11-08 UTC)

该段8项可定位日期记录

1952 17–20 Oct 1952: A U.S. ambassador's top-secret cable reports a demonstration near the Palace/Parliament and a subsequent petition by senior Army officers led by Nasution seeking dissolution of Parliament and transfer of authority to the President. The ambassador says his central account came from President's Secretary/Chef de Cabinet Pringgo Digdo, with limited distribution requested to protect him; the petition and several source telegrams were not printed. A separate 22 Oct cable records Sukarno's own explanation of the crowd and his reported statements of respect for Sultan Hamengkubuwono IX. These are attributed accounts, not a complete independent reconstruction. [S104–S105]

1954 2 Mar 1954: The Department of State's Office of Intelligence Research issued a secret estimate, prepared for NIE-65-54, that described the 1952 affair and intra-Army tension between small professional-army advocates and those favoring retention of a larger force including revolutionary guerrilla/irregular elements. The report is evidence of U.S. intelligence analysis, not neutral ground truth. [S103]

1955 29 Sep and 15 Dec 1955: KPU's official historical table gives separate election totals for DPR and the Constituent Assembly; PKI is listed at 6,179,914 votes, 16.36%, 39 DPR seats, and 6,232,512 votes, 16.47%, 80 assembly seats. The four largest party lists in the KPU DPR table are PNI (8,434,653; 57 seats), Masyumi (7,903,886; 57), NU (6,955,141; 45) and PKI (6,179,914; 39); for the Constituent Assembly they are PNI (9,070,218; 119), Masyumi (7,789,619; 112), NU (6,989,333; 91) and PKI (6,232,512; 80). A 2018 regional-government monograph prints a 3,000-lower DPR PKI figure (6,176,914), citing Feith. The original PPI tally and relevant ANRI items remain to inspect. [S099–S101; C078–C079]

1956 20–21 Jul / 1 Dec 1956; 5 Feb 1957: Later publications quote Hatta's letters as linking resignation to the elected DPR and Constituent Assembly taking office and to his view that a separate vice-presidency was unnecessary under a parliamentary cabinet; later institutional scholarship also points to differences with Sukarno. Keppres No. 13/1957 was issued in February but took effect from 1 December. Keep constitutional explanation, political difference and formal effective date distinct. [S107–S108; S113]

1956 26 Dec 1956: An embassy telegram records Hatta's reported conversations with Simatupang and leaders of several parties during the Sumatra crisis, along with personal context about his wife's illness and desire for a quieter life; it reports that Hatta favored Sukarno setting up a presidential cabinet. This is the ambassador's paraphrase, not a transcript or definitive private motive. [S106]

1957 14 Mar and 17–27 Dec 1957: Keppres 40/1957 declared the entire territory including territorial waters in a state of emergency/war on 14 March. Separately, Keppres 225/1957 on 17 December declared the whole territory in a state of war under the 1957 emergency law, and Law 79/1957 ratified it on 27 December. Law 74/1957 furnished a statutory framework; do not collapse the March and December instruments into one act. [S108–S111]

1959 16 Dec 1959: Perpu 23/1959 revoked Law 74/1957 and established civil-emergency, military-emergency and war levels; it gave the President/Supreme Commander and emergency authorities specified powers over public meetings, publications, movement, property and detention. This establishes legal authority on paper, not how or where each power was used. [S112]

1958 14 Apr 1958: A secret U.S. State Department profile linked Nasution's early officer training, Army professionalization, close rapport with U.S. observers, and family/political conflict with Zulkifli Lubis. Its reported cousin relationship is an archival lead; its psychological and causal claims about hostility are low-confidence until checked against Indonesian family and military records. [S114]

Fifth-pass source and access note

The ANRI sourcebook Jejak Demokrasi Pemilu 1955 was found and its official release notice and downloadable PDF were opened. The file's text layer is corrupted in relevant sections, and screenshot extraction failed because the file exceeded the browser tool's content limit; therefore it is recorded as an archive gateway rather than treated as read evidence. The BPS election table also appeared in search results, but direct access returned 403 and its indexed row may be mislabeled. Both limits and follow-up routes are logged.

Eighth-pass 1963–1966 addendum: source-specific chronology

该段18项可定位日期记录

1963 13–18 Mar 1963: In a conversation recorded by Australia, Subandrio alleged Malayans had helped the 1958 rebellion and supported Sumitro and Des Alwi. This is an attributed allegation, not proof of the claimed financial/media ties. [C096; S122]

1965 6–8 Jun 1965: A U.S. Medan consul reported Darjatmo invoked PEPELRADA/DWIKORA power to override a provincial press decision concerning Tjahaja. The local orders remain to locate. [C098; S124]

1965 15 Jul 1965: Australian ambassador Shann argued in a secret cable that Sukarno was widely revered and his government was not Communist, despite leftist tendencies. Record as his policy analysis. [C095; S121]

1965 13 Sep 1965: ANRI speech finding aid item 798 identifies a Sukarno speech at Aidit's Bintang Mahaputra III ceremony. Catalogue only; speech and personal implications unreviewed. [C090; S117]

1965 1 Oct 1965 morning: Untung's public broadcast claimed an internal Army action and alleged a CIA-sponsored Council of Generals. These are movement assertions. [C081; S115]

1965 1 Oct 1965 afternoon: The movement's No.1 decree claimed its Revolution Council held state authority and sidelined the Dwikora Cabinet. This shows a change/difference in its public constitutional claim, not actual control. [C082; S115]

1965 1 Oct 1965: Sukarno's published order claimed temporary direct Army/Armed Forces leadership, appointed Pranoto to daily Army affairs and told units to stay in place. Exact issuance/broadcast/receipt timing unsettled; the later Keppres 302 independently references the order. [C083; S115–S116]

1965 2–3 Oct 1965: Sukarno's second message assigned security restoration to Suharto while retaining Pranoto for daily affairs; a later 3 Oct message denied Air Force involvement and called his Halim visit voluntary. The latter are Sukarno's own assertions. [C084–C085; S115–S116]

1965 4–5 Oct 1965: Coalition associated with NU, PSII, IPKI and Catholic Party demanded party bans, asset seizures, censorship and purges. Separately, PKI Politburo's 5 Oct statement denied party involvement and said listed party figures had not consented; its printed date was 8–9 Oct. Both are public positions, not proof of underlying private facts. [C087; C089; S115]

1965 8–18 Oct 1965: Australian cable conveyed a British/Ghazali account of a purported 8 Oct Indonesian Army request for Malaysian propaganda support. Multiple layers of hearsay; direct contact unverified. [C094; S119]

1965 14 Oct 1965: Keppres 302 formally revoked 1 Oct temporary Army arrangement, relieved Pranoto's daily role and appointed Suharto Minister/Army Commander. [C086; S116]

1965 16 Oct 1965: ANRI item 816 identifies a Sukarno speech at Suharto's appointment ceremony; full text still to inspect. [C090; S117]

1965 23 Oct 1965: National Security Archive's description of U.S. A-298 says Adnan Buyung Nasution reported executions and concealment from Sukarno. Original OCR unreadable; any conclusion about Sukarno's actual knowledge remains open. [C099; S123; S125]

1965 28–31 Oct 1965: Hasluck instructed Critchley to discourage escalated Borneo operations for the benefit of Indonesian Army capacity in its domestic struggle; an editor's note reports an Oct31 reply about Malaysian easing instructions. Direct Australian instruction, later reply and field implementation are different evidentiary steps. [C092–C093; S120]

1965 Oct 1965–mid 1966 in Aceh: Jess Melvin reports internal military records for an Oct1 command chain, Oct4 civilian declarations, public killings from Oct6 and later military-controlled killing sites; her quoted death-map count is 1,941 public deaths, not the complete Aceh total. Her ~10,000 total extrapolation is explicitly speculative. [C102–C103; S127]

1965 30 Nov 1965: U.S. Embassy weekly report described mass executions in multiple provinces and attributed some in Central Java apparently to Suharto's behest. Embassy intelligence/assessment, not a signed Indonesian order. [C100; S126]

1966 11–12 Mar 1966: An ANRI-hosted archival study says the three Supersemar copies ANRI held were not the original dispatched paper; non-original does not mean forged. A U.S. Embassy cable characterized the power change as a military coup with Sukarno remaining visible and reported a PKI ban by Suharto; this is a U.S. assessment. [C104; C106; S128; S130]

1966 5 Jul 1966: MPRS XXV affirmed the 12 March 1966 PKI dissolution decision and prohibited dissemination/development of Communist/Marxist-Leninist teaching. Its accusatory preamble is the assembly's statement. [C105; S129]

1965 After 1 October 1965: ANRI's 2025 BTI guide reports KOTI seizure of PKI/BTI office archives in Jakarta, Bekasi and Tangerang; the seizure collection later passed through state secretariat and arrived at ANRI in disorder. Reprocessing and a surviving 50 BTI boxes do not establish a complete survivor sample. [S133; C109]

1964 17–30 April 1964: ANRI BTI no.262 lists resolutions demanding dissolution of KORAMIL and PEMBINA. This is a document locator, not proof that the demands were accepted. [S133; C113]

1964 17 May–30 July 1964: ANRI BTI no.269 lists discussions of aksi sepihak; no.270 covers East Java actions including Nov–Dec results lists and a Kediri regent's PPN land report through Jan1965. [S133; C114]

1964 5 Nov 1964–26 Aug 1965: ANRI BTI no.257 lists military-training material. No.258 contains March–July 1965 arming/training correspondence including DPP instruction 8/I/1965; no.259 separately records HANREV training at Lubang Buaya, 25–28 July. Bodies still unread; no immediate operational conclusion. [S133; C110–C112]

1965 Jan–Feb 1965: Zhou's analysis of PRC diplomatic files describes Chinese small-arms offers and Indonesian interest; acceptance in principle must not be conflated with arrival. [S134; C118]

1965 June–13 Sep 1965: Andoko asked about Chinese delivery to the Air Force in June; Dani on 13 Sep discussed 25,000 arms from an overall discussed 100,000. [S134; C118]

1965 5 Aug 1965: Zhou reports an Aidit-Mao conversation on political contingencies and a proposed military committee, quoting a restricted CCP record shared with her; text of the entire original is not independently available here. [S134; C122]

1965 17 Sep 1965: Zhou records a Zhou Enlai–Dani meeting in Beijing but says its minutes remained unavailable; a later recollection of a request to deliver weapons and limited Halim militia arms support a historian's no-large-shipment inference, not a conclusive customs inventory. [S134; C119]

1965 26 Oct 1965: The U.S. Embassy's telegram 1184 nevertheless asserted that several thousand Chinese guns had been smuggled and given to Communist paramilitaries. Its allegation is not interchangeable with physical receipt evidence. [S135; C120]

1966 27 Apr 1966: A U.S. Hong Kong consular telegram, in the NSA curator's synopsis, traced one highly specific Mao-directed-coup story in army press to a December 1965 Hong Kong satire. Distinguish exposure of that false narrative from broader foreign-relationship questions. [S136; C121]

1967 1 Mar 1967: McNamara's memo reports 1950–65 U.S. MAP to Indonesia totalling $63.2m, with more than 100,000 small arms included in Army provision and about 2,100 personnel trained in the U.S.; assessment of political effects is McNamara's view, not an operational trace to particular 1965 killings. [S137; C123]

Tenth research pass chronology — 2026-11-08, source-level entries

该段15项可定位日期记录

1965 19–22 Oct 1965 (Central Java): Hammer dates RPKAD's entry into Semarang (19 Oct), transit via Yogyakarta/Boyolali and reach of Surakarta (22 Oct). This is a scholarly reconstruction from other research and oral records, not an independently accessed deployment log. [S139; C126]

1965 23 Oct 1965: National Security Archive curator describes U.S. Embassy telegram 1168 regarding army action against SOBSI and Labor Minister Sutomo; Ambassador Green reportedly thought SOBSI involvement in G30S improbable while seeing its independent power as central to army goals. Curator description only. [S146; C138]

1965 29 Oct–1 Nov 1965: Komnas HAM gives date 29 Oct for inspection-team order KEP-1196/10/1965 and 1 Nov for KOTI/PANGTI 142/KOTI/11/1965. The order chronology is possibly inconsistent; do not infer existence/status of Pangkopkamtib command on 29 Oct without original texts. [S140; C130]

1965 15 Nov–21 Dec 1965: Hammer cites KOTI 22/1965, KOSTRAD Kep-069/10/1965, Kep-70/11/1965 and Kep-1/Kopkam/12/1965 as stages of detention screening and widening persecution criteria. Exact chronology and addressee to inspect in primary originals. [S139; C127]

1965 26 Nov 1965 (East Java): Surabaya consul relay reported attacks and murders affecting rail labor and ludruk theater. The reported 15,000 Tulungagung deaths were doubted by the author; never treat as established toll. [S145; C136–C137]

1965 10 Dec 1965 (Pati/Kudus, Central Java): the two-page A-386 facsimile signed by Embassy First Secretary Robert J. Martens was read directly. It relays Catholic Party coordinator Suhandjo's account that RPKAD sought cooperation from Catholic, IP-KI and NU organizations in Kudus; Ansor attacks extended to alleged non-PKI personal-feud victims; and Suhandjo had furnished the Army a list of over 200 Baperki members, while a separate 20-leader list was due for questioning and many people were said to have disappeared. This is a single informant's report, not a preserved list, order or verified individual-fate record. The local list is not evidence of U.S. supply. [S151; C143; C648; C649]

1965 21 Dec 1965 / 25 Feb 1966 / 1970 (Bali estimate conflict): A-408 archive synopsis reports the embassy's estimate of at least 10,000 deaths in Bali; the FRUS d162 editorial note says A-527 later estimated 80,000 and relays Howland's 1970 estimate of 6,000 in Bali and 105,000 nationally. These different source chains and methods are not additive; the A-408 scan was not visually checked and Howland's original was not read. [S400; S401; C708; C786]

1965 28 Dec 1965: NSA description of Surabaya telegram 212 says killings had become more discreet and Madiun detainees reportedly passed to civilian groups. Original requires inspection. [S152; C144]

1966 Jan–Jun 1966 (Roti/Sawu): Fox's account to U.S. Embassy in August describes several army visits and deaths; his wider 800–1,000 estimate is not for all East Nusatenggara including Flores. OCR incomplete and screenshot failed. [S143; C135]

1966 4 Mar 1966: U.S. Ambassador Green's A-673, per NSA curator, doubts China as primary force behind G30S while describing some anti-Chinese charges as politically manufactured. [S150; C142]

1966 21 Jun 1966: MPRS IX resolution officially indexed; this is legislative confirmation stage associated with March presidential order, not the date of the March 11 event. Full resolution unexamined. [S141; C132]

1966 29 Jun 1966: U.S. Embassy A-808 describes loyalty screening of civil servants, according to NSA curator; original and Indonesian personnel forms pending. [S149; C141]

2008 2008–2012: Komnas HAM’s Ad Hoc team says it examined 349 witnesses/victims and visited some areas but could not investigate every region or hear all witnesses; its own report cites geographic scale, budget and the passage of time. It records local anti-Chinese targeting in Makassar but supplies no national ethnicity-coded victim table. Its legal-category counts are not a national death census. [S859;C2356;C2682–C2683]

1967 22 Feb–12 Mar 1967: MPRS XXXIII/1967 was dated 12 Mar and, according to official abstract, retroactive to Feb22, withdrawing Sukarno's governing powers and installing acting president Suharto. Political accusations in the legislative materials are allegations. [S142; C133]

1969 3 Mar 1969: Keppres 19/1969 promulgated a reorganized Kopkamtib structure including LAKSUS, budgeting and use of state apparatus, revoking an earlier 179/KOTI/1965 decree. It cannot be projected unchanged backward into 1965. [S144; C134]
