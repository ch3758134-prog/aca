## Confirmed high-priority findings for further verification

1. Sukarno's formation and private/political relationships require records beyond summary biographies. The Oetari/Inggit marriage dates conflict across institutional and scholarly summaries.
2. The 1942–45 occupation requires separate treatment of nationalist agency, Japanese institution-building, coercive labor and famine; no simple exoneration or single-person responsibility is established.
3. Madiun in 1948 is both an actual armed conflict and a contested causal narrative; primary accounts, U.S. reporting and later scholarship should remain separate.
4. U.S. conduct varies by period and channel: postwar diplomatic/economic leverage over the Netherlands, covert aid to regional dissidents in 1957–58, anti-PKI political action and proposed/implemented Army assistance in 1964–66. Each operational chain requires recipient-side confirmation.
5. Foreign relationships with China, the Soviet Union, Japan, Britain and Australia are only partially mapped; earlier aid/alliance does not prove involvement in the September 1965 events.
6. Agrarian and labor laws do not by themselves demonstrate implementation or explain local violence. Regional land, workplace, religious and military records are needed.
7. Event responsibility, mass-violence mechanisms, death toll, Supersemar versions and transfer of authority remain unresolved. No final political judgment is entered.
